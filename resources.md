# Resources and Practice Plan

## Official starting points

Use official sources for current role descriptions and process instructions:

- [Palantir Careers](https://www.palantir.com/careers/)
- [Palantir Open Positions](https://www.palantir.com/careers/open-positions/)
- The instructions supplied directly by your recruiter

Role descriptions are valuable preparation inputs. Extract the users, deployment environments, technical responsibilities and judgment signals instead of merely collecting keywords.

## Repository practice sequence

### If your interview is in 48 hours

1. Read the [interview map](interview-process/fdse-interview-map.md).
2. Memorise the sequence—not the wording—of the [decomposition framework](decomposition/decomposition-framework.md).
3. Deliver the [sample problem](decomposition/sample-problem.md) aloud.
4. Review the [OOD logistics example](object-oriented-design/logistics-example.md).
5. Score one answer with the [rubric](scorecards/self-assessment-rubric.md).

### If your interview is in seven days

- Days 1–2: problem framing and decomposition
- Days 3–4: coding, OOD and failure reasoning
- Day 5: customer and adoption case
- Day 6: full mixed practice and independent scoring
- Day 7: targeted remediation and light review

## Further study topics

- API and data-contract design

- Event time, ingestion time, idempotency and correction
- Optimistic concurrency and transaction boundaries
- Observability, rollback and degraded operation
- User research, workflow mapping and adoption measurement

## How to evaluate external interview reports

Candidate reports can help identify competency categories, but they are incomplete and age quickly. Before relying on one, ask:

- Is the role, level, geography and business area comparable?
- Is the report recent enough to be relevant?
- Does it describe one experience or claim a universal process?
- Does it reveal confidential question content that should not be redistributed?
- Can the underlying competency be practised without memorising the question?

Use reports to decide **how to practise**, not to predict an exact prompt.
